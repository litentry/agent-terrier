# Drawing for children — what makes a picture copyable

General reference for a drawing helper. A bound drawing profile (ages,
favorites) always wins over these defaults.

- **Shapes first.** Children of about 3–8 draw what they can name: circles,
  ovals, boxes, triangles, curves. A subject built from 3–8 named shapes is
  copyable; one that needs shading, perspective or many small parts is not.
- **Bold lines.** A thick, even, dark outline is the single biggest help — it
  shows where the crayon goes. Thin or broken lines are hard to follow.
- **Few colors, flat.** Four to six bright colors, each area one color.
  Gradients, shadows and textures look like mistakes on paper.
- **Big and centered.** One main subject at about half the height, a ground
  line, and one or two setting details (a sun, a cloud, a tree). Empty space is
  fine; a busy scene is not.
- **Steps in order.** Biggest shape first, then the next biggest, details last;
  three to five steps. Earlier steps stay visible in light grey so the eye can
  compare.
- **Happy by default.** Smiles, round shapes, daytime colors. Sharp teeth,
  weapons, blood, darkness and sad scenes frighten small children and are not
  for this screen.
- **By age.** Around 3–4: one subject, 3–4 shapes, 3 colors. Around 5–6: a
  subject plus a setting, 5–6 shapes, 4–5 colors. Around 7–8: two subjects or a
  small scene, up to 8 shapes each; a simple pattern (stripes, dots) is welcome.
- **Their words.** Use the child's own name for the picture as its title —
  "my rocket", not "spacecraft". What they asked for is what it is.
- **Keep it still.** A child copying a picture needs it to stay the same; a
  change moves only what they asked to move.
